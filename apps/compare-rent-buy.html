How this works

A fair comparison

Both paths have exactly the same money available. On day one, the buyer pays the down payment and the purchase costs, and the renter invests that same sum. Each month, each path pays its housing costs. The buyer pays the mortgage, upkeep, property tax, insurance and building fees. The renter pays rent. Whichever path is cheaper that month invests the difference. Once the mortgage is paid off, owning usually becomes the cheaper path, so the buyer starts investing too.

Net worth means "if you sold today"

Buy: home value minus selling costs minus the remaining mortgage, plus any investments after tax. Rent: investments after tax on gains. Profit on a main home is assumed to be tax-free, which is the usual rule in Belgium and most of Europe.

Uncertainty

The headline numbers assume your inputs hold exactly every year. Real markets are noisy. The odds and the range come from 1,000 simulated futures. In each one, stock returns and home prices vary year to year around your expected values, using the volatility under Advanced, and are mildly correlated (ρ = 0.2). Rents partly follow home prices: half of a home-price surprise feeds into that year's rent increase, because a housing boom also pushes rents up. The simulation uses a fixed random seed, so the same inputs always give the same result.

Defaults

  • Home prices +3%/yr, inflation 2%: over the long run, home prices in most of Europe have grown only slightly faster than inflation.
  • Investments 7%/yr: roughly the long-run nominal return of a global stock index (about 5% after inflation). The calculation then subtracts fund costs and tax on gains.
  • Maintenance 1%/yr of home value: a common rule of thumb. Use more for older houses and less for apartments where the building fees cover the shared parts.
  • Purchase costs 6%: roughly what you would pay for a home you live in, in Flanders, including the mortgage deed. Brussels and Wallonia cost more (see the hint beside the field).

What's not modelled

  • Mortgage-interest tax relief: it is gone in Flanders, Brussels and Wallonia. Rental deposits, moving costs and big renovations are also left out.
  • Rates that change: the mortgage is treated as fixed-rate for its full term.
  • The money isn't everything. Security, freedom to move, and a home you can change all matter. If buying costs you €100/month more but you value staying put more than that, buying can still be the right call.
  • Discipline: the renter only comes out ahead if they actually invest the difference. Lower Share actually invested under Advanced to see how much that matters.

This is a model, not financial advice. Use it to see which assumptions matter, and stress-test the ones you're least sure of.
